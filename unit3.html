Protein Structures and Prediction Algorithms

How a sequence becomes a shape, and how computers predict that shape.

Unit 3 of 56 sessionsCLA-2
Session 1

Protein synthesis and primary structure

Transcription copies a gene from DNA into mRNA in the nucleus. Translation happens at the ribosome: each three-base codon on mRNA is matched by tRNA to one amino acid, building a chain until a stop codon. The order of amino acids joined by peptide bonds is the primary structure, and it alone determines how the protein folds.

In depth

  • Transcription: RNA polymerase binds the promoter (TATA box in eukaryotes), builds mRNA 5' to 3' on the template strand, stops at a terminator. Eukaryotic mRNA is processed: 5' cap, poly-A tail, introns spliced out.
  • Translation: initiation (small subunit finds AUG, codes methionine), elongation (tRNA enters the A site, peptide bond forms, ribosome shifts through P and E sites), termination (UAA, UAG or UGA, release factors).
  • Genetic code: triplet, degenerate (64 codons for 20 amino acids), universal, non-overlapping, no punctuation.
  • Primary structure is read N-terminus to C-terminus and decides the fold (Anfinsen). Mutations: silent, missense (sickle cell: Glu to Val), nonsense, frameshift.

Remember: 20 amino acids, 64 codons, so the code is redundant.

Session 2

Secondary, tertiary and quaternary organisation

Secondary: local patterns held by hydrogen bonds, mainly alpha helices and beta sheets. Tertiary: the full 3D fold of one chain, stabilised by hydrophobic packing, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges. Quaternary: several chains joined into one complex, like haemoglobin with four subunits. Each level builds on the one below.

In depth

  • Secondary: alpha helix (3.6 residues per turn, hydrogen bond from residue i to i+4) and beta sheet (parallel or antiparallel strands), plus turns and loops. A Ramachandran plot shows allowed phi and psi angles.
  • Tertiary: hydrophobic core, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, van der Waals forces. Chains may have domains. Chaperones help folding.
  • Quaternary: subunits assemble, e.g. haemoglobin (two alpha, two beta) and antibodies.
  • Types: fibrous (collagen, keratin, long and strong) vs globular (enzymes, compact). Misfolding causes prion disease and is linked to Alzheimer's.

Remember: Primary = text, secondary = formatting, tertiary = whole document, quaternary = bound book.

Session 3

PyMOL for 3D protein visualisation

PyMOL is software to view and render molecules from PDB files (the Protein Data Bank stores solved structures, each with an ID like 1TUP). You can show cartoon, stick, surface views, colour by chain or residue, measure distances and make images. Basic commands: fetch 1tup, show cartoon, color red, ss h, zoom.

In depth

  • The PDB database stores solved structures; each entry has an ID (e.g. 1TUP). A PDB file lists ATOM lines with atom name, residue, chain and x, y, z coordinates.
  • Common commands: fetch 1tup, show cartoon or sticks or surface, color by chain, select, label, distance, zoom, png to save an image.
  • Uses: locate the active site, measure hydrogen bonds (about 3 angstrom), superimpose two structures with align, view ligand binding, mutate a residue.
  • Alternatives: UCSF Chimera, Mol* in the browser. Free educational versions of PyMOL exist.

Remember: Seeing the active site helps understand how a drug or ligand binds.

Session 4

Secondary structure prediction methods

Goal: predict helix, sheet or coil for each residue from sequence alone. Early methods: Chou-Fasman (amino acid propensities) and GOR (statistics of neighbours). Later: neural networks (PSIPRED) using evolutionary profiles, reaching about 80 percent accuracy. Modern deep learning tools such as AlphaFold go further and predict the full 3D structure.

In depth

  • Generations: Chou-Fasman (residue propensities, 50-60 percent), GOR (looks at a window of neighbours), PSIPRED (neural network on evolutionary profiles, about 80 percent).
  • Input is the sequence (or multiple alignment); output is H, E or C for each residue. Accuracy is reported as Q3.
  • Tertiary prediction: homology modelling (SWISS-MODEL), threading, ab initio, and AlphaFold2 (CASP14, 2020), which predicts near-experimental structures. The 2024 Chemistry Nobel went to Baker, Hassabis and Jumper for protein design and prediction.

Remember: Input is a string of letters, output is a string of H, E, C labels.

Session 5

Python programming for structure analysis

Python with Biopython lets you parse PDB files, loop over chains and residues, read atom coordinates, count residues, and compute distances between atoms. Typical steps: parse the file with PDBParser, iterate model, chain, residue, atom, then use NumPy for calculations and matplotlib to plot, for instance a Ramachandran-style or distance plot.

In depth

  • Biopython workflow: install with pip, use PDBParser to get_structure from a file, then loop structure, model, chain, residue, atom.
  • Useful calls: atom.get_coord() for x, y, z; subtracting two atoms gives their distance; PPBuilder extracts the sequence; DSSP assigns secondary structure.
  • Analysis ideas: count residue types, find atoms within 4 angstrom of a ligand, compute radius of gyration, inspect B-factors (flexibility), plot with matplotlib and NumPy.
  • Structure is just nested data: model, chain, residue, atom with coordinates.

Remember: Structure is just a nested list of atoms with x, y, z numbers.

Session 6

Basics for drug design

Drug design finds molecules that bind a target protein and change its action. Structure-based design uses the 3D target and docking software to test how a ligand fits; ligand-based design uses known active molecules. Key ideas: binding site, affinity, selectivity, then lead optimisation and ADMET (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, toxicity) checks, followed by clinical trials.

In depth

  • Pipeline: target identification and validation, hit discovery (high-throughput or virtual screening), lead optimisation, preclinical tests, Phase I-III trials, approval. It takes 10-15 years and over a billion dollars.
  • Structure-based: dock ligands into the binding pocket (AutoDock Vina) and rank by score. Ligand-based: pharmacophore and QSAR from known actives.
  • Lipinski rule of five for oral drugs: mass under 500, logP under 5, at most 5 H-bond donors, at most 10 acceptors.
  • Success stories: captopril (ACE), HIV protease inhibitors, imatinib. ADMET filters remove toxic or poorly absorbed molecules early.

Remember: Docking = trying keys in a lock and scoring the fit.
